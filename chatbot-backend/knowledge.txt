============================================================ 1. COMPANY
OVERVIEW ============================================================

Company: CarbonRoot

Positioning: Building India’s Next-Gen Carbon Credit Infrastructure.

Core message: Driving India’s Green Economy: Empowering Rural
Communities with Technology.

CarbonRoot is presented as a comprehensive, bottom-up carbon origination
platform designed to connect India’s smallholder farmers with the global
voluntary carbon market.

============================================================ 2. MISSION
============================================================

CarbonRoot’s mission is to empower India’s smallholder farmers by
building the nation’s first bottom-up carbon origination platform,
directly linking them to the global voluntary carbon market and ensuring
fair carbon revenue distribution.

============================================================ 3. VISION
============================================================

CarbonRoot envisions a future where Indian villages become key
contributors to the global carbon economy, minting carbon credits and
unlocking India’s carbon potential while empowering climate-vulnerable
populations.

The deck also describes the vision as a future where Indian villages do
not just grow crops, but actively mint carbon credits, allowing climate
action and rural prosperity to develop together.

============================================================ 4.
CARBONROOT’S SOLUTION
============================================================

CarbonRoot’s full-stack platform is designed to:

-   Efficiently onboard smallholder farmers.
-   Use technology to generate low-cost, verifiable carbon credits.
-   Connect farmers to the global voluntary carbon market.
-   Provide up to 70% of carbon-credit revenue directly to farmers,
    according to the company deck.

The solution has four major components:

A. AGGREGATION CarbonRoot aggregates small and marginal farmers through
Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs), Self-Help Groups (SHGs), and
cooperatives. Aggregation provides the scale needed for effective
carbon-market participation.

B. VERIFICATION CarbonRoot’s technology stack uses: - Satellite
imagery - IoT sensors - Mobile-first data collection - Local-language
interfaces

The objective is to deliver low-cost, verifiable carbon credits.

C. TRACEABILITY Blockchain-based tracking is described as enabling
transparency from farm to buyer and helping build trust in the quality
of Indian carbon credits.

D. CONNECTION CarbonRoot plans direct integration with international
carbon markets and identifies VERRA and Gold Standard as target/global
registry connections. The deck states that this approach is intended to
maximize value and ensure up to 70% of revenue directly reaches farmers.

============================================================ 5. THE
PROBLEM CARBONROOT ADDRESSES
============================================================

The deck identifies four major barriers to India’s participation in the
voluntary carbon market:

FRAGMENTATION Millions of smallholder farms struggle to monetize carbon
credits because of limited scale and inadequate infrastructure.

EXPENSIVE MRV Monitoring, Reporting, and Verification processes are
described as costly, slow, and opaque for individual farmers.

TRUST DEFICIT Global buyers may lack confidence in the quality and
verifiable nature of Indian carbon credits.

UNFAIR DISTRIBUTION The deck states that over 80% of carbon revenue
fails to reach farmers who implement sustainable practices.

Overall problem: India’s carbon mitigation potential remains
significantly undersupplied to the global voluntary carbon market,
leaving substantial potential revenue unrealized.

============================================================ 6. WHY
CARBONROOT / WHY NOW
============================================================

MARKET GROWTH The deck cites a projection that the global Voluntary
Carbon Market could reach $250 billion by 2050, attributed to McKinsey.

POLICY MOMENTUM The deck highlights India’s commitment to net-zero by
2070 and ongoing domestic carbon regulations as factors supporting the
carbon-credit ecosystem.

CORPORATE DEMAND The deck states that more than 4,000 global
corporations with science-based net-zero targets are actively seeking
reliable and impactful voluntary carbon credits.

TECHNOLOGICAL READINESS The deck points to the convergence of: - Farmer
aggregation platforms - Advanced satellite MRV - Secure blockchain tools

as making the CarbonRoot approach scalable and technically feasible.

============================================================ 7. MARKET
OPPORTUNITY ============================================================

The deck describes India’s current carbon-market participation as
minimal and says it is primarily driven by large corporations and urban
projects.

Existing carbon-credit generation is described as being largely
concentrated in industrial and renewable-energy projects, while rural
potential remains underutilized.

The deck identifies millions of smallholder farms as a major unexplored
source of India’s carbon potential.

MARKET FIGURES FROM THE DECK: - Indian agriculture/forestry mitigation
potential: 2.5 billion tCO2e by 2030. - Estimated tradable credit
supply: approximately 500 million tCO2e. - Target carbon price for
removal/high-quality credits: $10–20+ per tCO2e. - Addressable India
market by 2030: $5–8 billion annually. - The deck states that unlocking
rural agriculture and forestry could generate $5–8 billion annually by
2030.

============================================================ 8. PRODUCT
/ PLATFORM OVERVIEW
============================================================

CarbonRoot describes its product as a B2B2C technology platform.

The platform consists of four major components:

A. FARMER APP The Farmer App is designed for: - Farmer enrollment -
Land-record uploads - Tracking sustainable practices -
Vernacular-language support - Offline functionality - Simplified data
input for rural users

B. AGGREGATOR CONSOLE The Aggregator Console is designed for FPOs and
cooperatives to: - Manage bulk farmer onboarding - Pre-validate
sustainable practices - Visualize MRV data across member farms

C. MRV ENGINE The MRV Engine uses: - Satellite NDVI data - AI-powered
yield inference - AI-powered soil-carbon inference - Blockchain-based
smart contracts

The deck describes the system as supporting tamper-proof verification of
carbon sequestration.

D. BUYER PORTAL The Buyer Portal is intended to allow global buyers
to: - View certified carbon credits - Assess co-benefit ratings -
Purchase credits - Track impact

The deck identifies co-benefits including: - Biodiversity - Water
conservation - Gender equity

The platform is engineered around transparency, speed, and
verifiability.

============================================================ 17. COMMON
QUESTIONS — APPROVED ANSWERS
============================================================

Q: What does CarbonRoot do? A: CarbonRoot is building next-generation
carbon-credit infrastructure for India. Its full-stack platform is
designed to aggregate smallholder farmers, support technology-driven
MRV, create verifiable carbon credits, provide traceability, and connect
projects with global voluntary carbon markets.

Q: Who does CarbonRoot work with? A: The platform is designed around
smallholder farmers and rural communities and uses FPOs, SHGs and
cooperatives for aggregation. The deck also identifies NGOs, CSR
projects, agricultural cooperatives, international buyers and potential
institutional partners as part of the broader ecosystem.

Q: How does CarbonRoot help farmers? A: CarbonRoot aims to connect
smallholder farmers to the voluntary carbon market, simplify onboarding
and data collection, and direct up to 70% of carbon-credit revenue to
farmers according to the company deck.

Q: What is MRV? A: MRV means Measurement, Reporting and Verification. In
CarbonRoot’s platform, MRV is supported by satellite NDVI data,
AI-powered yield and soil-carbon inference, IoT data, mobile-first data
collection and blockchain-based systems.

Q: What technology does CarbonRoot use? A: The deck identifies satellite
imagery, NDVI data, IoT sensors, AI-powered yield and soil-carbon
inference, mobile-first data collection, vernacular-language interfaces
and blockchain-based tracking/smart contracts.

Q: What is CarbonRoot’s Farmer App? A: It is designed for farmer
enrollment, land-record uploads and sustainable-practice tracking. It
supports vernacular languages, offline functionality and simplified data
input for rural users.

Q: What is the Aggregator Console? A: It is a dashboard designed for
FPOs and cooperatives to manage bulk farmer onboarding, pre-validate
sustainable practices and view MRV data across member farms.

Q: What is the Buyer Portal? A: It is a marketplace interface intended
to let global buyers view certified carbon credits, assess co-benefit
ratings, purchase credits and track impact.

Q: What are CarbonRoot’s revenue streams? A: The deck identifies a
15–25% commission on carbon-credit sales, MRV-as-a-Service
subscriptions, marketplace commissions, and premium credits with
co-benefits.

Q: How much revenue goes to farmers? A: The deck states that up to 70%
of carbon-credit value is directed to farmers implementing sustainable
practices.

Q: Which regions will CarbonRoot focus on first? A: The Year 1 plan
focuses on Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh and Odisha, with a target of
partnering with 10 FPOs and covering more than 100,000 acres.

Q: What is CarbonRoot’s market opportunity? A: The deck estimates an
addressable India market of $5–8 billion annually by 2030, with 2.5
billion tCO2e of Indian agriculture/forestry mitigation potential and
approximately 500 million tCO2e of tradable credit supply.

Q: What is CarbonRoot’s investment ask? A: The deck states that
CarbonRoot is seeking INR 75 Lakhs (~$90,000) in pre-seed capital for
team expansion, pilot deployments, registry onboarding and legal
infrastructure.

Q: What will the investment be used for? A: The stated uses are team
expansion, MRV pilot deployments across three states, registry
onboarding/certification work, and legal/certification infrastructure.
